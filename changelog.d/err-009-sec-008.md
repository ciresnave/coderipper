kind: Feature: two new opt-in rules
---
**ERR-009 and SEC-008 are built: a secret named in an error message or a log record (Rust, opt-in).** One scan reads the tracked `.rs` files outside tests with `syn` and reads the tokens of each macro directly. **SEC-008** (no sensitive data in logs or telemetry) reports a logging macro (`error!`, `warn!`, `info!`, `debug!`, `trace!`, `log!`, `event!`, the `tracing` span macros) that names a sensitive value, Medium severity. **ERR-009** (errors and logs leak no sensitive data) reports the same reading for error-message macros (`anyhow!`, `bail!`, `ensure!` without its condition, `eyre!`, `format_err!`, `panic!`), for a `format!` inside `Err(..)`, `.context(..)`, `.with_context(..)`, `.map_err(..)`, `.ok_or(..)`, `.ok_or_else(..)` or `.expect(..)`, and for `write!`, `writeln!` or `format!` inside the `Display` impl of a type named `...Error` or `...Err`, High severity. Both are Low confidence: the reading is of names, not of data flow. A name is sensitive when its last word is `password`, `passwd`, `passphrase`, `secret`, `token`, `credential(s)`, `cookie`, `authorization`, `bearer` or `apikey`, or its last two words are `api_key`, `private_key`, `secret_key`, `signing_key`, `access_key`, `session_key`, `auth_key`, `master_key` or `encryption_key` (`token_count`, `token_type`, `tokenizer`, `has_password` are not). It counts as a variable or field in the arguments, a named `tracing` field (`token = ?t`) or an inline format capture (`"bad {token}"`); it does not count as the receiver of `len` or `is_empty`, as the base of a plain field read (`auth_token.user_id`), when passed through or called with a redacting name (`redact`, `mask`, `hash`, `digest`, `fingerprint`, `obscure`, `hide`, `scrub`, `sanitize`, `anonym`, `censor`, `safe`), as the name of a call, or as a named field given a literal. Both are opt-in (`coderipper check ERR-009`, `coderipper check SEC-008`): a secret behind a name that does not say so, a `{:?}` of a whole struct that holds one, a type that redacts itself on display and a log made by a wrapper are not seen. Fixtures `conformance/{ERR-009,SEC-008}/{defective,clean}`; with `info` and `bail` renamed in the macro lists both conformance runs fail, and restored both read proven. Measured on committed trees of eight of our repositories (2026-11-10): SEC-008 reports 2 sites in 2 repositories and ERR-009 reports 1 site in 1 repository; the other repositories report none. Of the 3 sites, 1 is a protocol constant and 1 is a parser token, neither of them a secret, and 1 is a real leak that was handed to its owner privately and is not described here. The first reading, before the plain-field and redacting-method exclusions, reported 40 SEC-008 sites in one repository, nearly all a field read of a token object. Three sites are too few to claim the rules' precision.
