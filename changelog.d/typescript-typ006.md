**TypeScript `TYP-006`: a non-null assertion (`value!`) with no stated invariant** (ROADMAP items 8 and 12, second rule of the TypeScript module). One Medium finding per file at high confidence, naming the first site and the count; a `// INVARIANT`, `// SAFETY` or `// UNWRAP-OK` comment on the same line or the line above justifies a site, as in the Rust rule. `!==` and a definite-assignment `value!: T` are not sites. Opt-in (named), as for Rust, because it fires widely. Proven by a seeded defect and a clean twin in `conformance-typescript/TYP-006`. Measured at 8c52ed5 on the committed trees of four of our own TypeScript repositories (files read, files flagged): 295 and 33, 94 and 24, 24 and 1, 3 and 0. Of five flagged sites read by hand, all five are real `!` assertions on an indexed or looked-up value (`rows[0]!`, `map.get(k)!`); `bytes[i]!` inside a bounded loop is the pattern the rule names and an owner may call it justified, which is why the rule stays opt-in. No false-positive rate is claimed from five sites. Not built: the other TypeScript rules and JavaScript files.
